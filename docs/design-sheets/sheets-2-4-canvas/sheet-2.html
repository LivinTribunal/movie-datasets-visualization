Operations

1. Pick a genre in B. Map A recolours to that genre's share, and D draws the same genre.

2. Click countries in A (up to 6). Each one adds a bar pair in C and a line in D. Click again to remove it. A search box in F covers small countries.

3. Hover a country. Tooltip: share, rank, source, and how many films or chart weeks the number rests on.

4. Switch the map mode under A: share of the genre (sequential), top genre (categories), or cinema − streaming (diverging, PuOr).

5. Switch the source in F. Netflix chart weight, box office or admissions. Every view relabels its unit.

6. Brush years in D. The year range in F follows, and A and C recompute for that window.

7. Hover a segment in C. That genre is highlighted in B and D.

Focus

Big idea: the map is the way in. You start from where films are watched, and every other view answers "and what about this country?"

Share of genre: sequential Blues, 5 classes. Hatched = no data, never a colour from the scale.
Top genre: 8 families (Okabe–Ito) plus a 3-letter label on large countries, so colour is not the only cue.
Cinema − streaming: diverging PuOr centred on 0 pp. Orange = more in cinemas, purple = more on Netflix. Only countries that have both sources.

The tooltip always shows how many entries a share is based on, and the source. A country with fewer than 30 entries gets a dotted outline, so you don't read too much into noise (lecture rule: say when data is thin or transformed).

Discussion

Supports
  • T1 and X2 directly: regional patterns are visible at a glance.
  • A familiar way in for a casual viewer: click your own country first.
  • C shows the exact mix for a few countries, which the map cannot.
  • D adds T4 per country, not just worldwide.
Misses
  • No single films, so T2, T3, T5 and T6 are not covered.
  • Large countries dominate the map visually and small ones are hard to click.
  • The map shows one genre at a time; comparing all genres needs C.
  • Netflix chart weight and box office mean different things, and only some countries have both.
