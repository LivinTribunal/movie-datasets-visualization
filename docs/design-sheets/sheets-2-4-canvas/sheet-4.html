Operations

1. Brush years in A. B, C and D keep only franchises whose first film falls in the brushed years.

2. Switch the measure in F. A redraws the shares by releases, IMDb votes or real box office.

3. Hover a genre band in A. Franchises of that genre stay coloured in B and the rest turn grey. Its row in D is highlighted.

4. Search or click a franchise in B. Its line is coloured and labelled, and C draws its path through the installments.

5. Switch y in B between rating and revenue, both relative to film #1 (±0 line). The median band shows the typical sequel.

6. Click a row in D. Filters B to that genre, so you can see the franchises behind the percentage.

7. Hover a point in C. Tooltip with the title, year and scores, and the money with its source.

Focus

Big idea: time on the x axis. Taste changes across decades (A) and across installments (B, C), and the year brush ties the two together.

Fallback for A: if 100 % shares hide how big a genre really is, A becomes small-multiple lines with a shared y axis.
Relative y: every franchise starts at 0, so "better or worse than the original" is read straight off the position. Raw scores show in the tooltip.
Clutter control: all franchises are thin grey context lines, and only the ones you search, hover or pick get colour and a label.

Discussion

Supports
  • T4 and T6 directly, plus T2 inside one series (C).
  • D gives a one-line answer to "do sequels get worse", and B shows the exceptions.
  • Relative y makes franchises of different quality comparable.
  • Reads like a story: good for the video demo.
Misses
  • No countries, so T1 and X2 are missing.
  • T3 (critic–audience gap) is not shown.
  • 100 % areas hide absolute sizes; the fallback costs space.
  • Franchise membership comes from TMDB collections, which miss some series and mix in reboots.
