Operations

1. Pick a genre in F or click a legend swatch. There is one selected genre, and every view shows it.

2. Click countries in A (up to 6) or search them in F. B, C, D and E then show only films and charts from those countries.

3. Zoom and pan A. Scroll, the +/− buttons, or double-click a country to zoom to it. This covers small countries.

4. Switch the map mode under A: share of genre, top genre, cinema − streaming. It recolours A only.

5. Brush years in B. The year range in F follows, and every view recomputes for that window.

6. Brush in C. The brushed films fill table E.

7. Choose X ▾ and Y ▾ in C. Any measure goes on any axis. The same field twice is greyed out. Money and votes go log on their own.

8. Pick a franchise in D or F. Its line is coloured in D and its films are joined in C, in installment order, on whatever axes C has.

9. Sort or add columns in E. Hover a row or a point to fill the film card G.

Focus

Big idea: one screen, one store: every view filters every other view. Nothing hides behind a tab.

One selected genre. The map mode only changes colour. In top-genre mode a click on a legend swatch selects that genre, and F and B follow. The tooltip says both: “top genre: Drama · selected (Action): [XX] %”.
Free axes. X ▾ and Y ▾ list every measure. Only the same field on both axes is blocked. A franchise is read on whatever axes C has, so D needs no axes of its own, only a Y ▾ for rating or revenue.
The shaded table. In-cell bars and PuOr-shaded gap cells, sortable by any column, columns picked with ▾. LineUp.js is the model. We draw the bars with D3 scales, so there is one dependency less.

The tooltip always names both genres and says how many entries a share rests on. A country with fewer than 30 entries gets a dotted outline (lecture rule: say when data is thin or transformed).

Detail

Data. Films: Released, 1900–2026, ≥ 1,000 IMDb votes; money views use ≥ 10,000. TMDB + IMDb snapshot 2026-10-01; Netflix top 10 up to 2026-09-27; box office per country (Box Office Mojo); cinema admissions (LUMIERE); Rotten Tomatoes, Metacritic, Letterboxd scores; money from TMDB, The Numbers, Wikidata, Wikipedia; CPI + FX to 2025 USD. A Python (polars) pipeline writes static files for the app.
Shown as transformed. “2025 USD” label and a source badge on every money value; scores rescaled to 0–100; fuzzy-matched titles marked; thin data (< 30 entries) gets a dotted outline; “[N] films hidden: no budget”.
Technology. React + TypeScript + Vite, D3 (scales, geo, brush, zoom), a zustand store shared by all views, scatter on canvas (~25k points), the rest SVG. Static build, runs locally and on GitHub Pages.
Colours. 8 Okabe–Ito swatches, Blues ramp, PuOr ramp, hatch. Checked with a colour-blindness simulator.
